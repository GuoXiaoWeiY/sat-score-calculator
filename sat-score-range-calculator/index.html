Why a range is more honest than one number

The Digital SAT is module-adaptive and each test form is equated separately. Two students with the same number of correct answers can receive different scaled scores depending on which second module they were routed into and how that form was equated. A calculator that takes only correct-answer counts cannot see either factor. Showing a band is a way of saying what the tool actually knows.

Where this band comes from

We ran our conversion function point by point against two publicly published Digital SAT tables and compared every output. The largest differences were 30 points in Reading & Writing and 40 points in Math; across all tested points the mean absolute error was 11.7 and 20 points respectively. Adding the two worst cases gives a total-score band of about 70 points.

Those are differences between published tables, not a promise about your personal score. We did not compare estimates with individual students' official score reports. The sources, every data point, and the exact method are in the measured accuracy report.

How to use the range

  1. Take a full-length practice test in Bluebook when you can; that is the most realistic score you will get before test day.
  2. Enter your total correct answers for each section above to get a planning range.
  3. Track the range across several practice tests. A shift larger than the band is more likely to be real progress than noise.
  4. Never use an estimated range as an admissions credential. Only College Board issues official scores.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SAT score range calculator?

A score range calculator gives an estimated score plus a band instead of a single false-precision number. Our band is based on measured differences between published conversion tables.

How wide is the band?

Up to 30 points for Reading & Writing, 40 for Math, and about 70 for the total. These are table-to-table differences, not a personal confidence interval.

Why can the same raw score produce different scaled scores?

Because the Digital SAT is adaptive and forms are equated. A published dual-track table shows the same raw count mapping to scores 170–200 points apart between Hard and Easy Module 2 paths.

Can I use this range for college applications?

No. Only College Board issues official SAT scores. Use this tool for study planning only.
