Why your grade changes your percentile

This is the most useful thing on the page, and almost every PSAT percentile chart online buries it. Your percentile is a rank within your grade, not against all test takers. Because 11th graders have finished more math and have more testing experience, they score higher as a group — so a given score falls lower in their distribution.

  • A 1100 is roughly the 69th percentile in 11th grade, the 82nd in 10th, the 90th in 9th and the 95th in 8th.
  • A 1200 is the 82nd for juniors but the 92nd for sophomores — a ten-percentile swing from one year of school.
  • A 1000 is about average for a junior (53rd) but comfortably above average for a sophomore (69th).

Practically, that means two students with the same PSAT score can be in very different positions. A sophomore at 1100 is on a strong track; a junior at 1100 is only slightly above the junior mean. Never compare your percentile to a chart that doesn't name the grade it belongs to.

Rule of thumb: use the 11th-grade column if you are a junior, because that is the grade National Merit and most PSAT conversations assume. Underclassmen should read their own grade's column and expect the number to drop as they move up a grade, even if their score rises.

National Merit: it's the Selection Index, not your total

Here is the single most common PSAT mistake: assuming a high total score wins National Merit. It doesn't. The program uses a separate number called the Selection Index, computed from your two section scores with Reading and Writing double-weighted:

Selection Index = (2 × Reading and Writing + Math) ÷ 10, on a scale of 48 to 228. For example, 700 R&W and 700 Math gives (2×700 + 700) ÷ 10 = 210.

Because R&W counts twice, a student with 750 R&W and 650 Math (index 215) outranks one with 650 R&W and 750 Math (index 205), even though both total exactly 1400. Only the PSAT/NMSQT taken in 11th grade counts — a higher score in 10th grade does not qualify you.

Turning a PSAT percentile into an SAT target

A PSAT percentile is a useful starting point for planning because the two tests use the same question types, the same timing and the same adaptive format. But they are different scales and different comparison groups, so treat the PSAT as a readiness signal rather than a prediction.

  1. Find your grade's column in the chart above and note your percentile — for a junior, a 1200 at the 82nd percentile is a solid but not elite position.
  2. Convert to a rough SAT starting point. The PSAT tops out at 1520 and the SAT at 1600, and a well-prepared student usually gains some points by test day. A 1200 PSAT often maps to a similar or modestly higher SAT score once preparation is done.
  3. Set the real goal from college data, not from percentile. Look up the middle-50% SAT range at your target schools and aim above the 75th percentile of admitted students; our good SAT score guide walks through that.
  4. Model the study time. The SAT score predictor takes your current PSAT or practice score, weeks until the test and weekly hours, and returns an estimated score range with the assumptions shown.
  5. Re-test on a real SAT practice exam. Bluebook full-length tests are free and are scored on the SAT scale, so they are the honest way to confirm your starting point.

If you are aiming for National Merit, work backwards from the Selection Index instead: boost Reading and Writing first, because each R&W point counts twice toward the index that actually decides recognition.

Four things PSAT percentiles do not tell you

1. Percentile is not percent correct

A 82nd percentile does not mean you answered 82% of questions right. The test is equated across dates, so the same percent-correct can produce different scaled scores on different forms. Percentile is a rank against students; percent correct is a property of your answer sheet.

2. It is not an admission number

Colleges do not use PSAT scores for admission and generally do not see them on the standard score report. A strong PSAT percentile is a planning signal and, in 11th grade, a scholarship signal — nothing more.

3. It is not comparable to the old PSAT

Before 2023 the PSAT used a different scale and structure. Older percentile tables and "good PSAT score" advice circulating online often refer to the pre-2023 test and will mislead you against today's 320–1520 scale.

4. It is a snapshot, not a label

College Board recomputes percentiles each cycle from the most recent testing population, and the grade you are in changes the number anyway. A two-point move is noise; a ten-point move across a grade boundary is expected. Judge progress with section scores and practice-test trends instead.

Frequently asked questions

What percentile is a 1200 PSAT score?

It depends on your grade: about the 82nd percentile for an 11th grader, 92nd for a 10th grader, 96th for a 9th grader and 98th for an 8th grader. Always match the number to the grade column.

What is the average PSAT score?

About 1000 for 11th graders and 917 for 10th graders in the 2025-26 school year, per College Board's 2026 annual report. Medians were roughly 980 and 900.

Is the PSAT scored out of 1600?

No. The PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 10 run from 320 to 1520, with two sections each scored 160 to 760. The PSAT 8/9 tops out at 1440. Only the SAT uses the 400 to 1600 scale.

What PSAT score is needed for National Merit?

National Merit uses the Selection Index, (2 × R&W + Math) ÷ 10, not your total. For the class of 2027 the Commended cutoff was 208 nationally and Semifinalist cutoffs ranged from 208 to 223 by state.

Does a good PSAT percentile predict a good SAT score?

It is a reasonable readiness signal because the tests share question types and format, but they are different scales and populations. Confirm with a full-length SAT practice test before setting a target.

Are PSAT percentiles the same every year?

No. They are recalculated each cycle from recent test takers, so small shifts of a point or two are normal and not meaningful on their own.

Do colleges see my PSAT score?

Generally no. Colleges do not use PSAT scores in admission and do not receive them through the standard score report. Its jobs are practice and National Merit qualification in 11th grade.
