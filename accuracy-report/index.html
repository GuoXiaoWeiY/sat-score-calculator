How accurate is our SAT score estimator?

What we actually measured

Our calculator takes the number of questions you answered correctly in each module, adds them (54 total for Reading & Writing, 44 for Math), converts that raw count to a 200–800 scaled score using a fixed interpolation curve, and adds the two section scores. The curve is:

0→200, 10%→270, 20%→340, 30%→410, 40%→470, 50%→530, 60%→590, 70%→650, 80%→710, 90%→770, 100%→800, interpolated linearly and rounded to the nearest 10.

We ran every raw-score point from a published reference table through that exact function, then compared our output with the reference value. Positive error means we over-estimated; negative means we under-estimated.

Sources (retrieved November 24, 2026)

  1. freesatcalculator.com — publishes separate raw-to-scaled tables for RW (out of 54) and Math (out of 44), described as "based on official College Board Digital SAT scoring curves for 2025–2026." We use it as the primary comparison set.
  2. gradesnova.com/exams/sat — publishes Hard Module 2 and Easy Module 2 conversion tracks, stated as derived from Bluebook practice tests 1–6 (2024–2025). We use it to show why one curve cannot cover both paths.

Both sources are third-party publications. We have not independently verified them against primary College Board score data. Treat the reference numbers as published estimates, not official answer keys.

Why no single curve can be exact

The Digital SAT is adaptive: your Module 1 result routes you to an easier or harder Module 2, and each path has a different score ceiling. A published dual-track table shows the same number of correct answers mapping to very different scaled scores:

For Math (out of 40 scored), the median gap is 170 points and the maximum is 200. Our calculator asks only for total correct answers, so it cannot tell which path you were on. Even a perfectly calibrated single curve would still be wrong by up to 200 points for some students. That is a structural limitation, not a coding bug.

Note the different scoring convention: this dual-track source uses 50 RW / 40 Math scored questions, while our calculator uses all 54 / 44 displayed questions because students cannot identify unscored pretest items. The two tables are not directly interchangeable.

What these numbers do and do not mean

  • They are real and reproducible. Every comparison point, the curve, and both sources are on this page. You can recalculate the error yourself.
  • They are not an official accuracy rating. We compared against published tables, not against official score reports from real test-takers.
  • They are not a prediction interval. We do not claim "within ±N points" for any individual result. We removed that claim from the calculator after this measurement.
  • Sample size is small. 12 RW and 10 Math reference points from one table set. Different published tables would produce different numbers.
  • The tool is for tracking, not predicting. Its best use is comparing your own estimates across several practice tests to see whether a section is improving.

What we will change next

  1. Correct the Math curve in the low-to-middle range, where the under-estimate is largest (raw 5–20, currently −30 to −40 points).
  2. Add an optional Module 2 difficulty selector (Hard / Easy / Not sure) so the estimate can account for the adaptive path.
  3. Identify the specific Bluebook practice test number when possible, and use that form's conversion data instead of one blended curve.
  4. Publish a second validation pass against a different published table set, and update this page with the result.

Until those changes ship, use the calculator as a rough progress tracker and confirm important decisions with official College Board score reports.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official College Board accuracy study?

No. It is an independent comparison against two publicly published third-party tables. We have not compared estimates with real official score reports, so these numbers are not a guaranteed personal error margin.

How accurate is the SATCalcPro estimator?

Against the tables tested: 11.7 points mean absolute error in Reading & Writing and 20 points in Math. Math also showed a systematic under-estimate averaging 16 points in the middle range.

Why can't one conversion curve be exact?

Because the Digital SAT is adaptive. The published dual-track table shows the same raw count producing scores 170–200 points apart on the Hard versus Easy Module 2 path. Our calculator only sees total correct answers.

Do you publish the raw data and method?

Yes. Every comparison point, the interpolation curve, both source URLs and the retrieval date are on this page. The calculation can be reproduced independently.

Can I use the estimate for college applications?

No. Only College Board issues official SAT scores. Use this tool to track practice-test progress, not as an official score or admissions credential.
