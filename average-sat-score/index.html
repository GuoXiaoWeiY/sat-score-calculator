Average SAT Score in 2026

The average SAT total score has hovered around 1024–1050 in recent graduating classes. Here are the published numbers, why they moved, and what they mean for your target.

Quick answer: the national average SAT total score for recent graduating classes is roughly 1024–1029 (about 515–520 Reading & Writing, 500–505 Math). Anything above ~1100 puts you above the majority of test takers.

College Board publishes average scores for each graduating class in its Total Group reports. Note that each year's class is a different group of students — including a growing share who test during school days under state contracts — so averages reflect the test-taking population, not a change in test difficulty.

Why did the average drop after 2021?

During the pandemic many colleges went test-optional, so fewer students took the SAT — and the ones who did were often self-selected, stronger test takers. As states added universal in-school SAT testing, the population expanded to include students who might not have chosen to test, pulling the average down. The drop reflects who takes the test, not a harder exam.

What the average means for you

  • Average describes all test takers — not the admitted students at selective colleges, whose averages are far higher (1400–1550).
  • If your practice scores sit near 1000–1050, you're in the middle of the pack nationally; targeted prep can move you well past that quickly.
  • Compare yourself to your target colleges first, not the national number. See what counts as a good score and the full percentile chart.

Track your own average

Take a full practice test every few weeks, estimate each result with the calculator, and chart the trend. A moving average over 3+ tests is far more meaningful than any single sitting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average SAT score in 2026?

For recent graduating classes the average total score is about 1024–1029. College Board publishes updated averages for each graduating class in its Total Group reports.

Is 1050 above average on the SAT?

Yes, slightly — around the 40th percentile. It is close to the national average but below the typical admit range at most four-year colleges.

Why is the average lower than it used to be?

More students now take the SAT through universal in-school state testing, which broadens the test-taking population. Averages describe the whole population, not a change in test difficulty.
