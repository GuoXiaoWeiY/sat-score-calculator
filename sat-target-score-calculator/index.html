How the target score calculator works

Our SAT score calculator converts correct-answer counts into a scaled score. This page runs the same curve in reverse: it searches for the raw counts that produce a section-score pair closest to your target total. The output is an approximate study goal, not a promise.

Two assumptions matter. First, it balances Reading & Writing and Math evenly. If one section is much stronger for you, your real requirement will be different. Second, it uses one conversion curve, while the adaptive Digital SAT can route two students with the same raw counts to different scaled scores. Published dual-track tables show gaps of 170–200 points between Hard and Easy Module 2 paths.

How to use your target

  1. Pick a target based on the middle-50% range of schools you are considering.
  2. Use this page for a rough correct-answer goal in each section.
  3. After each full practice test, enter your real counts into the score calculator and compare with the target.
  4. If your range is wider than the difference between your current score and the target, focus on the section with the larger gap.

Frequently asked questions

Can you calculate SAT raw scores from a target score?

Approximately. This tool inverts the same conversion curve used by our calculator and estimates the correct-answer counts needed for a target total, under an equal-difficulty assumption.

How many questions do I need right for a 1400?

Under the balanced assumption, roughly 42 of 54 in Reading & Writing and 34 of 44 in Math. Real requirements vary with module routing and test form.

Is this official?

No. College Board does not publish one universal raw-to-scaled table for every Digital SAT form. This is a study-planning aid only.

Should I set separate section targets?

Yes if you have a clear weakness. This tool balances sections evenly; you can adjust the target by improving the weaker section more.
