What Is a Good SAT Score in 2026?

A "good" SAT score depends entirely on where you want to go. This guide gives you concrete 2026 benchmarks by college type, so you can set a real target instead of guessing.

Quick answer: a good SAT score is one that sits at or above the middle-50% range of your target colleges. For most state universities that means 1200+; for selective private schools 1400+; for top-20 colleges you want 1500+.

The SAT is scored from 400 to 1600, combining two 200–800 section scores: Reading & Writing and Math. The national average is around 1030–1050, so any score above roughly 1100 already beats the majority of test takers. But "good" is relative — a 1250 can be excellent for one school and below average for another.

Good section scores

Admissions officers look at section scores too. A balanced profile (e.g. 700 R&W + 700 Math) reads better than a lopsided one for most programs. Engineering and STEM programs often want Math 700+; humanities-leaning programs weigh Reading & Writing more heavily.

How to set your personal target

  1. Pick 3–5 colleges you'd be happy to attend.
  2. Find each school's middle-50% SAT range on its admissions page.
  3. Take the highest bottom edge of those ranges — that's your floor target.
  4. Take a practice test and use the SAT score calculator to see your baseline.
  5. Retake practice tests monthly and track the trend, not a single result.

What if my score is below the range?

Many schools are test-optional in 2026, and a strong GPA, essays, and activities can offset a lower score. But if you do submit, aim to be inside or above the middle-50% range — a below-range score can hurt more than omitting it. Note that some public flagships are moving back to requiring scores; always check the current policy for each school.

Frequently asked questions

Is 1200 a good SAT score?

Yes for most public universities and many private colleges — it sits around the 72nd percentile nationally. It is below the typical admit range for top-30 schools.

Is 1400 a good SAT score in 2026?

Yes. 1400 is around the 90th percentile and falls inside the middle-50% range at selective universities like Georgia Tech and University of Michigan.

Do colleges look at section scores or just the total?

Both. They see your 200–800 section scores plus the 400–1600 total. STEM programs often check Math specifically, so a balanced profile is usually safer.
