How percentiles work

An SAT percentile compares you to the college-bound population, not to the whole U.S. population of your age. Two percentile types appear on your score report:

  • Total score percentile — your 400–1600 score vs. all test takers.
  • Section percentiles — your 200–800 Reading & Writing and Math scores compared separately.

Because scores cluster near the middle of the range, percentile jumps are biggest around 1000–1200 and smallest at the extremes: moving from 1000 to 1100 gains you about 18 percentile points, while moving from 1500 to 1550 gains only 2.

Using percentiles to set a target

  1. Find the middle-50% range for your target colleges.
  2. Convert the bottom edge of that range to a percentile goal.
  3. Take a practice test, estimate your scaled score, then look up your percentile here.
  4. Repeat monthly — percentile movement is easier to feel than raw point movement.

Frequently asked questions

What percentile is a 1500 SAT score?

Approximately the 97th percentile — you scored equal to or better than about 97% of test takers.

What percentile is a 1200 SAT score?

Approximately the 64th percentile. That is above average and competitive at many state universities.

Is the percentile the same for each section?

No. Section percentiles are calculated separately for Reading & Writing and Math, so the same 700 can have slightly different percentiles in each section.
