How is my SAT score calculated?

The digital SAT has two sections: Reading & Writing (RW) and Math. Each section is scored from 200 to 800, and the two are added together for your total score of 400 to 1600. Your raw score — the number of correct answers — is converted to a scaled score through a process called equating, which accounts for small differences in difficulty between test forms. There is no penalty for wrong answers on the digital SAT, so guessing is never worse than leaving a question blank.

How this calculator works (methodology)

Want to know exactly what happens when you press calculate? Here is the full method, in plain language.

  1. Add your correct answers in each section. Reading & Writing has two modules of 27 scored questions each (54 total); Math has two modules of 22 scored questions each (44 total).
  2. Convert each raw total to the 200–800 scale. We map your number of correct answers to a scaled section score using a score-conversion table drawn from publicly available official digital SAT practice tests, with linear interpolation between points.
  3. Add the two section scores. Reading & Writing + Math = your estimated total out of 1600, rounded to the nearest 10.

What this model does and does not account for: it uses the number of correct answers across both modules. It does not simulate the digital SAT's module-level adaptive routing (where your Module 1 performance decides whether you get the easier or harder Module 2), and it cannot account for the difficulty of the specific questions you saw. Because of this, treat the result as a close approximation, not an exact score.

Is this SAT score calculator accurate?

This tool gives an estimate, not an official score. College Board uses equating to adjust scores between test forms, and the digital SAT's adaptive modules mean two students with the same number of correct answers can receive slightly different scaled scores depending on question difficulty. Our calculator uses published score ranges from official practice tests, so it is close enough to guide study decisions and track your progress between practice tests — but expect a possible difference of roughly 30–60 points from an official result.

Is there a guessing penalty?

No. The digital SAT removed the old wrong-answer penalty. You earn credit only for correct answers, and wrong or blank answers both score zero. That means you should always answer every question, even when you have to guess.

What is a good SAT score?

It depends on the colleges you are targeting. The national average is around 1050. A score of 1200 or above is competitive at many state universities, while 1400 or above is strong for selective schools. There is no fixed point value per question on the digital SAT. Roughly, each correct answer in Reading & Writing is worth around 10–20 scaled points, and in Math around 10–30, but this is only an approximation.
