How College Board scores the Digital SAT

College Board converts your raw score (number of correct answers) into a scaled score between 200 and 800 for each section, then adds them for a total of 400 to 1600. The conversion uses a process called equating, which adjusts for small difficulty differences between test forms. On the Digital SAT, modules are adaptive: your Module 1 result determines the difficulty of Module 2, and harder modules can produce different scaled scores for the same raw count.

Using official practice-test score tables

The score tables released with College Board practice tests (on Bluebook) show the raw-to-scaled conversion for a specific form. Our calculator uses a smooth approximation of the ranges seen across those published tables, with linear interpolation between points. That makes it a useful planning tool, but not a substitute for the exact score report from your test.

Why your estimate may differ from the official score

Three factors cause differences: (1) adaptive module routing — we cannot know which module difficulty you received; (2) equating — College Board adjusts scores per test form; (3) unscored pretest questions mixed into each module. Expect a possible gap of roughly 30–60 points from your official result.

Frequently asked questions

Is this calculator affiliated with College Board?

No. This is an independent estimating tool built from publicly available practice-test score ranges. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or officially connected to College Board. Only College Board can issue an official SAT score.

Can I use College Board score tables to predict my score?

Yes, approximately. The tables published with official practice tests map raw correct-answer counts to scaled scores for a specific form. Our calculator smooths those published ranges into one estimator, so it is close but not exact.

Does College Board penalize wrong answers on the Digital SAT?

No. The Digital SAT removed the wrong-answer penalty. Only correct answers earn points, so guessing is never worse than leaving a question blank.
