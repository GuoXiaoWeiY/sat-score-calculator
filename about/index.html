About this calculator

Last updated: November 2026

This site does one thing: it turns the number of questions you got right on a Digital SAT practice test into an estimated 400–1600 score, and tells you how that score compares to other test takers. It is a single static page with no accounts, no uploads, and no server-side processing of your answers.

Who runs it

This is an independent project built and maintained by a solo developer. It is not a company, not a test-prep brand, and not affiliated with the College Board. The code and page content live in a public GitHub repository, so the scoring logic is open to inspection — if you think a number looks wrong, you can read exactly how it was computed.

Where the numbers come from

The Digital SAT is scored on a curve, not a fixed percentage: the same raw number of correct answers can produce different scaled scores on different test forms. What we publish is an estimate built from the conversion tables released with College Board practice tests, interpolated into a smooth curve across the 200–800 range for each section.

In practice this means:

  • For a typical practice test, the estimate usually lands within roughly ±30 points of the official score.
  • Very high and very low raw scores are the most reliable, because the curve flattens at both ends.
  • Your official score can only come from the College Board. This tool cannot and does not replace it.

How the percentile figures are derived

Percentiles describe where a score sits relative to everyone else who took the test. We use the aggregate distributions published in College Board's annual reports and express them as "the share of test takers who scored below this score." Because these distributions shift slightly year to year, treat percentiles as a close approximation rather than an exact ranking.

What this tool deliberately does not do

  • No accounts. There is nothing to sign up for, and no email to hand over.
  • No data collection from the calculator. Your answer counts are processed in your browser and never transmitted. Only anonymous page-view analytics run on the site — see the privacy policy.
  • No paywalled results. The score appears immediately, with the arithmetic shown.
  • No guarantees. An estimate is an estimate. It is a planning aid for setting a target, not a prediction of your official result.

Editorial standard: when College Board changes the test format or releases updated scoring guidance, the relevant pages are revised and the sitemap date is updated. If you spot a number that looks out of date or inconsistent with an official source, please tell us — corrections are the main reason the contact page exists.
